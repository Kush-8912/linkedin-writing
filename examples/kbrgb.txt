Every RGB tool I tried on Linux said the command worked.
My keyboard stayed on its factory rainbow anyway.

My laptop is an Acer Predator Helios Neo 16S AI. On this generation, Acer moved the keyboard lighting off the WMI interface every Linux tool talks to, and onto a separate ENE KB5130 chip on i2c-HID. The firmware still accepts every WMI call and replies OK. Then it does nothing with it.

The community had already cracked the basic HID packet (credit to the predator-sense project). I built kbrgb on top of that. One Python file, zero dependencies, no kernel module. 29 animated effects, per-zone colors, theme sync, restore on boot, and the chip's own built-in effects triggered with a single write, so the CPU does nothing while they run.

While testing it I found two things nobody had written down.

The brightness byte goes from 0 to 100, not 1 to 15. Tools that sent 15 as "max" were running everyone's LEDs at 15%. I reported it and predator-sense shipped a fix.

The controller has no memory. Cut the power and it wakes up in rainbow wave again, which is why kbrgb restores your colors at boot.

I also got one wrong in public. I said the all-zones mask was broken. Someone else's probe report didn't match mine, and when I went back, my test packet had gone out at brightness 15, so I was measuring two things at once. The retraction is still in PROTOCOL.md with a line through the original claim. I'd rather people see the mistake than have it disappear.

Where it ended up:
→ 16 stars, packaged on the AUR and on PyPI
→ confirmed working by strangers on Nitro and Predator models, across Arch, CachyOS and Ubuntu
→ 3 PRs merged into other Linux-on-Acer projects, including Predator key support in DAMX

All of this started because I wanted to change a keyboard color.
